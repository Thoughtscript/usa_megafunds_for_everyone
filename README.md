## Definitions

1. **U.S. Fund** - a **Fund** associated with, statutorily defined to operate within, a specific recognized, incorporated, geographic region (**Government Unit**) of the United States of America.
1. **General Purpose Fund** - a **Fund** with few statutory or regulatory constraints on use.
    * Cannot be statutorily defined *solely* as a **Budget Stabilization Fund**, **Emergency Facility**, or **Rainy Day Fund**.
    * Cannot be purposed to *solely* support a specific demographic (e.g. - pensioners, descendents, residents, etc.)
    * Can therefore be used to proactively pay down governmental debt, subsidize business incentives, support social spending, etc.
    * Must be capable of growing independently of or from tax revenue.
1. **Positive Growth Fund** - a **Fund**, endowment, or trust whose growth doesn't represent oustanding liabilities.
    * E.g. - a traditional 401K, Hedge Fund, etc.
    * Not a fund representing maturing and issued bonds.
    * Bonds can be used to facilitate investment (cash is moved from the sale of bonds into the **Positive Growth Fund** and the maturing bonds held in another, the former being used to underwrite the latter).
1. **Megafund** - a **Fund**, endowment, or trust with a diverse portfolio of assets, investments covering equities, bonds, etc.
    * It cannot be funded primarily through intra-governmental obligations.
    * It cannot be primarily composed of financial obligations (like Social Security claims).
    * It must have value equivalent to or greater than the annual inlays of a specific governmental body.
1. **Quasi-Governmental** - a statutory authority established by law but is itself an independent, private, entity not directly run or managed by any specific governmental body.
    * Can be a consortium of public and private interests.
    * Examples: Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac.

***Megafunds for Everyone***
* A **U.S. General Purpose Positive Growth Quasi-Governmental Megafund**
* One or more existing at various governmental levels: City, Metro, County, State, Federal.
 * Example: a `$400 Trillion` lockbox used to pay down Federal debt, underwrite U.S. insurance, eliminate taxation, support critical social programs, pay off all student loans ever, backpay for pensioners, incentivize business with direct subsidies, etc.

## Data 2026

> Python, Jupyter Lab [here](./jupyter/PewDataLab.ipynb) (to view the Pew Dataset which is a little unreadable as is owing the very helpful amount of data).

* [Census Bureau 2026](https://data.census.gov/table/GOVSORGTIMESERIES.GS00ORG02?g=010XX00US_040XX00US27) estimates there are `91,729` **Total Local Government Units** (`GO0002` **Aggregate Description**).
    * OpenAI estimates that at most `.75%` of all **U.S. Government Units** have a **Rainy Day Fund**.
* All `50` **U.S. States** have **Rainy Day Funds** (Contingency, Disaster, Emergency, **Budget Stabilization Funds**).
    * According to [Pew 2026](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2026/10/26/states-rainy-day-fund-capacity-and-with-it-their-fiscal-flexibility-continues-to-decline) and [2018](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2018/10/11/how-rainy-day-funds-help-cities-prepare-for-revenue-volatility) no **U.S. State** has more than `$7.25 Billion` set aside.
    * A Median of about `50 Days` of unimpending funding in emergency or disaster scenarios.
    * No such **Fund** qualifies as a **General Purpose Megafund**.
* The Federal Government has a handful of [Securities-based regimes](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/08/12/key-facts-about-the-us-national-debt/):
    * None of these qualify as a **General Purpose Fund** (nor as a **Megafund**).
    * These are, in fact, a great portion of the Federal Debt itself. 
    * While these can "grow in size" - the greater the value, the more debt the U.S. Federal Government *actually owes*.
